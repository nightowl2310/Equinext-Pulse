=== WHAT THIS IS ===
"Equinext Pulse" is one page of a larger stock-market intelligence site called
Equinext, for Indian retail investors. This page shows NSE "Participant-wise
Open Interest" — i.e. how the big players in the F&O (futures & options) market
are positioned. The four players ("actors") are:
  Pro    = proprietary trading desks (the sharpest, fastest money)
  FII    = Foreign Institutional Investors (big foreign money)
  DII    = Domestic Institutional Investors (Indian funds)
  Client = retail crowd
Every morning at 8 AM the page shows what changed in their positions, so an
investor knows the lay of the land before markets open.

=== BRAND / TONE ===
Trustworthy fintech, calm and premium — like a morning briefing you read at your
desk. Default theme: LIGHT — off-white canvas (#F7F6F2), dark-ink navbar
(#12151C), one signature accent (teal #0EA5A4 or amber — pick one). Numbers:
green (#158A4E) for increases (+), red (#C0362C) for decreases (-). Type: a
refined SERIF for the big headline (editorial feel); a clean SANS with TABULAR
figures for all tables so numbers align. Keep it themeable (a dark variant
should be trivial to swap).

=== SCREEN STRUCTURE (top to bottom) ===
1) NAVBAR (dark ink, full width). Left: wordmark "Equinext Pulse". Then nav
   items as tabs — NOT a dropdown menu:
     - "Open Interest"  -> ACTIVE (accent underline/highlight)
     - "FII / DII Flows"   -> disabled, small "Soon" pill
     - "Option Chain"      -> disabled, small "Soon" pill
     - "Delivery Data"     -> disabled, small "Soon" pill
   (These future items are greyed/non-clickable.)

2) HERO. Big serif headline: "Everything you need to know before you invest —
   on your desk by 8 AM." A muted subline: "NSE participant positioning,
   refreshed every trading morning." A small timestamp chip on the right:
   "As of 8:00 AM IST · 15 Jul 2026".

3) SUB-SELECTOR (segmented control, 3 tabs): Daily | Weekly | Monthly.
   DEFAULT = Daily (selected). Show the Daily content as the visible state;
   include Weekly and Monthly as two additional visual states/frames-in-place
   (or a second and third copy of the content area) so all three can be seen.

4) BRIEF CONTENT AREA (this is the core — reproduce the layout below).

=== BRIEF CONTENT LAYOUT (applies to each sub-selector) ===
Top of the content:
  - A "context bar": the two dates being compared, e.g.
      Daily:   "15 Jul 2026 (Wed)  vs  14 Jul 2026 (Tue)"
      Weekly:  "15 Jul 2026 (Wed)  vs  09 Jul 2026 (Thu · expiry)"
      Monthly: "15 Jul 2026 (Wed)  vs  25 Jun 2026 (Thu · expiry)"
  - A HEADLINE CARD (hero of the brief): the single biggest move, big and bold,
    e.g. "Pro added index call longs  +163,663 contracts (+22.8%)".
  - A small muted note chip when relevant, e.g.
    "Client hidden — biggest move only 1.0x their typical (needs 1.5x)".

Then ACTOR CARDS (one card per actor shown, stacked). Each card:
  - Actor name as the card title (e.g. "Pro") + a one-line "book" summary shown
    as two chips:  Long +324,935 (+7.0%)   |   Short +292,822 (+6.8%).
  - A compact TABLE of that actor's biggest moves, columns:
       Field  |  Old -> New  |  Change (abs)  |  %  |  Note
    Color the Change and % green(+)/red(-). Right-align numbers, tabular figures.
    The "Note" is a short plain phrase (e.g. "adding index call longs").
  - Cards for actors marked "(shown for coverage)" get a tiny subtle label.

Then a RIGHT-HAND or bottom "READ" PANEL:
  - A horizontal GAUGE from -1 (Bearish, red) .. 0 (Neutral) .. +1 (Bullish,
    green) with a needle at the score. Label the tilt (e.g. "NEUTRAL tilt,
    score -0.04").
  - 3 signal bullets, each with a green "bullish" or red "bearish" chip, e.g.
    "Pro adding index call longs +163,663  [bullish]".
  - An "Action" one-liner (plain text).
  - A tiny disclaimer: "Positioning read only — not investment advice."

Bottom of content:
  - A "TOTAL (context)" strip, e.g. "Total Long Contracts +1,399,677
    (20,810,416 -> 22,210,093)".
  - A muted footnote: "Moves selected by absolute change (size); % shown
    alongside as context — OI is quantity-driven."
